Privacy policy

Last updated 21 October 2026. Applies to the VOD Blur extension for Firefox and Chrome, and to this website.

Short version: VOD Blur collects no data. It has no account, no analytics and no server. What it stores stays in your own browser.

What the extension stores

Only what you make with it, saved in your browser's own extension storage on your device:

  • The shapes you save: their position, shape, blur or color, and the address of the page, site section or site they belong to.
  • The presets you save: a name you choose and the same details of the shapes in it.
  • Your last-used settings, such as shape type, blur strength and color.

None of this is sent anywhere. You can see and delete it on the extension's overview page, and removing the extension deletes it all.

The overview page can export your shapes and presets to a file. That file is saved only where you choose to save it; the extension doesn't upload or share it. Importing a file only reads it into your browser's extension storage.

Page content and screenshots

To draw shapes, the extension runs on the pages you visit, but it doesn't read, collect or send their content. It only measures where the video sits on the page, so your shapes stay in place.

When you use the color picker, the extension takes a screenshot of the visible tab so you can pick a color from it. The screenshot only exists in memory while the picker is open and is thrown away as soon as you pick a color or press Esc. It is never saved or sent anywhere.

What we don't do

  • No analytics, tracking or telemetry.
  • No selling or sharing of data, because there is no data to share.
  • No remote code: everything the extension runs ships inside the extension itself.

This website

vodblur.com is a single static page. It sets no cookies, loads no third-party scripts or fonts, and runs no analytics. Our host, Cloudflare, processes basic request data such as IP addresses to deliver and protect the site, as any web host does.

Questions

VOD Blur is open source, so you can check all of this in the source code. Questions or concerns: open an issue on GitHub.
